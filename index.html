Corporate Director · Calgary, Alberta

Rod Graham, ICD.D

Independent director. Former chief executive.

Rod Graham serves on the boards of the Alberta Energy Regulator and the Prince Rupert Port Authority. He came to governance from the operating side: chief executive of Alberta companies in manufacturing, workforce accommodation and truck retail, and before that, a long career in energy private equity. His work has centred on operations, capital discipline, safety and Indigenous partnerships.

Executive career

Investor first, then operator

Seventeen years in Alberta energy private equity, then fifteen as chief executive of three Alberta companies: a turnaround, a transformation through a downturn and a wildfire, and a sale.

  1. 2021 – 2024

    Velocity Truck Centres Canada

    President and CEO · formerly First Truck Centres

    Led the largest group of Daimler Truck dealerships in western Canada, twelve locations across British Columbia and Alberta. Under his leadership the company was acquired by Velocity Vehicle Group, announced November 1, 2023, a decision he framed around aligned core values, a team-first philosophy, and the resources to better serve customers and employees. He remains Past President.

    SectorCommercial truck retail
  2. 2015 – 2020

    Horizon North Logistics Inc.

    President and CEO · Chairman 2012–2015 · Director from 2007 · TSX-listed

    Joined the board in 2007, became Chairman in 2012 and took the chief executive role in early 2015. Converted a decentralized holding company into one organization with a common mission, vision and values, with safety and diversity as core principles. Led the company through the 2016 Fort McMurray wildfire and the 2020 merger with Dexterra, then served as co-CEO and President, Modular Solutions, through the initial integration.

    • Ran roughly 10,000 workforce accommodation rooms across western Canada and Alaska.
    • Built modular permanent construction, with projects in Revelstoke, Vancouver and Fort McMurray, and acquired Karoleena Inc.
    • Seventeen First Nations joint ventures; about 12 per cent of 1,100 employees of First Nations descent.
    • Evacuated seven of eight camps around Fort McMurray in 2016 while maintaining world-class safety.
    • Moved sales from relationships to technical analysis of reserve profiles and production data.
    SectorWorkforce accommodation and modular construction
  3. 2010 – 2012

    ZCL Composites Inc.

    President and CEO · Director from 2002 · TSX-listed, Edmonton

    A director of the Edmonton-based, TSX-listed fibreglass tank manufacturer since 2002, he stepped in as President and CEO on October 1, 2010, succeeding Venence Côté. He led the company from near-bankruptcy back to strong profitability, investing $1.5 million of his own money in the business, and concluded the mandate in August 2012.

    SectorIndustrial manufacturing
  4. 2005 – 2010

    Northern Plains Capital Corporation

    Co-founder and Managing Director

    Co-founded and ran an energy-focused private equity firm after eleven years at ARC. Over seventeen years in Alberta-based private equity he played a key role in sourcing, investing in and growing more than sixty oil and gas, energy service and energy industrial companies, an apprenticeship in capital allocation and company building that he’d draw on later as an operator.

    SectorEnergy private equity
  5. 1990s – 2005

    ARC Financial Corporation

    Senior Vice President and Director · Earlier: Shell Canada, Peters & Co.

    Eleven years at ARC Financial, rising to Senior Vice President and Director; he credits the firm as the foundation of his career. He’d moved to Calgary in 1989 and started at Shell Canada, with a period at Peters & Co. It was on the investing side that he formed a lasting view: producers and service companies needed a better way of dealing with each other.

    SectorEnergy private equity

Rod Graham is a Calgary-based corporate director and former chief executive whose career has moved from energy private equity to running Alberta companies, and now to board service in energy regulation, port infrastructure and Indigenous economic opportunity.

Born in Oakville, Ontario, and raised in Waterloo and Thunder Bay, he moved to Calgary in 1989 and has built his career in western Canada since. He has spent more than three decades in and around the energy sector, first as an investor and then as an operator, and has contributed to twenty-two corporate and not-for-profit boards. He’s an advocate for pairing numeracy with literacy, and for taking courses well outside one’s own field.

Approach

How he works

Lean process, safety as a first principle, Indigenous partnership built as operating relationships, and an owner’s view of capital. The same four themes run through every mandate he has held, and through how he sees a board’s job.

Process before heroics

An Ivey exchange term in Japan introduced him to lean manufacturing. He’s applied it since, hiring from outside energy: a former Lexus quality manager, a Honda plant executive.

Safety as core principle

At Horizon North, safety and diversity were written into the company’s core principles. When the 2016 wildfire forced seven of eight Fort McMurray camps to evacuate, the safety record held.

Partnership in practice

More than fifty Indigenous relationships developed or overseen across western Canada. At Horizon North: seventeen First Nations joint ventures, and roughly twelve per cent of employees of First Nations descent.

An owner’s perspective

At ZCL he put $1.5 million of his own money into the turnaround. As a director he brings the same owner’s view to capital allocation and cost discipline.
